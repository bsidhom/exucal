## Findings

**How the model holds up.** The schedule, the loop, settling, the table and the memos are as the three devdocs sections describe them, sentence by sentence, with the seven exceptions below, and every example the claims and the two settings entries give behaves as stated when run. The facts, with where each is pinned:

- **The schedule.** `workingContexts` (`src/core/context.ts:210-225`) yields the display digits plus `GUARD_DIGITS` (ten), doubled while strictly below the ceiling, and then the ceiling: 18, 36, 72, 144, 288, 576, 1152, 2304, 4040 at eight digits; 1010, 2020, 4040 at a thousand; ten passes at one digit. `ceilingFor(L)` is `4 × (L + 10)` (`src/core/context.ts:188-190`), set once by `contextOf` from the precision limit (`src/settings.ts:814-826`) and the same in every working context; every other field is the user's, and the decimal-mode working digits are dropped from every derived context (`withoutWorking`, `src/core/context.ts:132-136`). Display digits above `L` make `4 × (p + 10)` exceed the ceiling and are a `RangeError` in `workingContexts`, a precondition the settings' clamp (`rangesOf`, `src/settings.ts:496-518`) keeps from being reached. In decimal mode the schedule is the user's context alone (`schedule`, `src/core/pipeline.ts:299-303`), and a built-in's call, a unit and a power the significand limit refuses are certified inside `step` through the same `workingContexts`, run in exact mode, up to the ceiling (`src/core/ball.ts:1109-1134`).
- **A pass's precision is not its grid.** A pass at `p` digits runs the kernel at `bitsFor(p) = ceil(p × log2 10) + 32` bits (`src/core/fixed.ts:104`, `src/core/fixed.ts:113-118`), and a settle or a kernel result lands on `workingDigits(p) = digitsFor(bitsFor(p))` digits (`src/core/context.ts:144-146`, `src/core/rounding.ts:60-65`): 28 at the first pass of eight (92 bits), 4,050 at the ceiling pass of 4,040 (13,453 bits). The `zero` bound `4e-4049` of `(2^(1/2))^2 - 2` is that grid's half unit (`src/core/pipeline.test.ts:310-336` asserts a bound of that order). The pages call the ceiling "the finest grid the app rounds onto, 4,040 digits"; the grid of the ceiling pass is about ten digits above it (finding 3).
- **The loop.** `resolve` (`src/core/pipeline.ts:334-361`) stops at the first pass whose result carries no assumption and whose every shown value is exact, certified at the display digits in the rounding mode, or of unbounded radius (`settled`, `src/core/pipeline.ts:368-374`; `conversionDone`, `src/core/pipeline.ts:407-418`: the ratio, each list term as settled, a function want's count). A result that is not done, or that assumed a condition, is kept as the best so far. The errors retried are an `unresolved` at any depth of `callFailed`, `inverseFailed` or `inverseMismatch`, an `undecidedCondition`, and the converter's `unresolvedWant` and `unresolvedBound` (`src/core/pipeline.ts:377-404`). Any other error ends the loop: with the error when no pass produced a result or the best result assumed a condition, else with the best result; at the ceiling, the best result or the last error. The `have` is evaluated once at the first working context when the `want` fails to parse, so that its failure is reported first (`src/core/pipeline.ts:465-474`). A list unit straddling zero is `nonpositiveUnit`, which `isListRefusal` (`src/core/pipeline.ts:618-626`) keeps out of the refusals, so it is not retried. The page's account (`devdocs/numbers.md:867-898`) matches all of it. Its parenthesis "the only such error a later pass can newly raise is a `tooLarge` marking the pass unaffordable" is modelled by `src/core/pipeline.test.ts:529-560` and fits the built-ins that refuse on their own counts at the working bits, whose need rises with the pass; it was not traced for a classifier verdict that moves from `unresolved` at the bound to a refusal after an earlier pass produced a result (question 7). `resolve` retries no `tooLarge` of either reach (`src/core/rational.ts:66`, `src/core/rational.ts:80-83`, `src/core/rational.ts:96`: a reach of `fits` or `exceeds`, with a `need`), since its predicates never read the error's fields; where a `tooLarge` is retried it is inside a node or a step (`powered` and `summed` in decimal mode, `src/core/evaluate.ts:1125-1185`; the settle-first sum and the retried product and quotient, `src/core/ball.ts:509-512`, `src/core/ball.ts:666-669`, `src/core/ball.ts:840-843`), never by the schedule.
- **Settling.** `settle` (`src/core/ball.ts:1014-1018`) leaves an exact ball as it is; `gridded` (`src/core/ball.ts:1021-1039`) leaves a zero midpoint, rounds an inexact one `halfEven` to the pass's working digits, returns the ball unchanged when nothing moved, and otherwise adds half a unit in the last working digit, a radius taken from the exponent and never formed (`shiftRadius(unitOf(mid, digits), -1n)`); where the rounding's exponent would pass `MAX_EXPONENT` it takes the `towardZero` rounding and adds a whole unit. It cannot fail in exact mode: `settled` (`src/core/ball.ts:1136-1143`) throws on anything but the decimal rounding's `overflow` and `underflow`. `1e-999999999999999999 / approx(3)` is `≈ 3.3333333e-1000000000000000000` at the first pass (run), as the page says. `settleDecimal` (`src/core/ball.ts:1052-1063`) rounds an exact ball, or an inexact one that certifies the context's digits, to those digits in the rounding mode, exact; otherwise the midpoint's rounding, zero for a ball containing zero, flagged `uncertified`, the flag carried (`carrying`); a rounded value past the bound is refused (`roundedDecimal`). `onGrid` (`src/core/ball.ts:1009-1012`) is the same rounding applied to an exact operand of the settle-first sum and of the retried product and quotient. `step` (`src/core/ball.ts:1109-1134`) is the one entrance: in exact mode one `compute` at the context, then `settled`; in decimal mode `compute` at each working context in exact mode until the ball is exact or certifies, retrying an `unresolved` by default and ending on any other error, and at the ceiling the last ball settled and flagged. Its callers are `stepQuantity` (`src/core/quantity.ts:89-115`) for every evaluator node, `settledInMode` (`src/core/pipeline.ts:595-601`) for the pipeline's quotients, and the outcome's reciprocal; `settle` is unexported; `powBall` on an inexact base settles after each product and each squaring (`src/core/ball.ts:953-962`). `#Settling` (`devdocs/numbers.md:929-976`) states all of this correctly.
- **The table.** `buildSystemTable` (`src/system-build.ts:75-90`) builds at `ceilingContext` (`src/core/context.ts:235-241`: the precision the ceiling, exact mode whatever the user's, no working digits) and holds one table keyed by the definitions' identity and the ceiling; the significand limit is left out of the key by design (`src/system-build.ts:11-16`); the display digits and the rounding mode never rebuild and another ceiling does (`src/system-build.test.ts:34-57`). `unitLookup` hands a unit out as built (`src/core/table.ts:194-198`) and the `unit` node steps it (`src/core/evaluate.ts:525-535`), so `turn` into `deg` certifies `360` at the first pass. The build is `pi`'s first use, through `deg`, `grad`, `turn`, `oersted` and `parsec` (`src/units.txt:241`, `src/units.txt:292`, `src/units.txt:354`, `src/units.txt:357`, `src/units.txt:414`): `piMemoBits()` is `undefined` before the first build and 13,709 after it (run), against the page's "none … at the table's build" (finding 6).
- **The memos.** `memoAtCeiling` (`src/core/constants.ts:196-207`) is the page's rule; `piBall` reads the ceiling from the context it is called with (`src/core/constants.ts:223-228`, `src/core/builtins/pi.ts:20-25`). The footprint at the default ceiling is `bitsFor(4040) + 256`, 13,709 bits, the page's "about 1.7 KB"; at the hard bound (`precisionLimitFor(2^20)`, 39,444 digits, a ceiling of 157,816) about 65.6 KB per constant, which "the largest it can be is set by the hard bound on the precision limit" covers.
- **The display-digits entry.** Run: `1 m` into `in` is `39.370079` at eight digits and `39.3700787402` at twelve, `39.3700787401` under `floor`, each `certified` (`≈`) at the first pass; `1 ft` into `in` is `exact` `12`; `1/3*3` is `exact` `1` in exact mode and `0.99999999` in decimal mode; `(2^(1/2) + 1e-20) - 2^(1/2)` takes two passes, as cancellation costs them. The range, 1 to the precision limit, is `rangesOf` (`src/settings.ts:504-508`); the default is `DEFAULT_DISPLAY_DIGITS` (`src/core/context.ts:149`).

**Where the pages differ from the code.**

1. **"Four times the precision limit plus ten" reads as `4L + 10`.** The ceiling is `4 × (L + 10)`, 4,040 at `L = 1,000`; the phrase parses as 4,010. Where: `docs/settings.md:112` ("four times this plus ten digits (4,040 by default)"), `docs/settings.md:259-260` and `docs/settings.md:271` (D8's section, question 4), `devdocs/numbers.md:834-837` ("four times the precision limit plus the guard, 4,040 digits"), the claim N8 itself, and in code the headers of `src/core/pipeline.ts:296-297` and `src/core/context.ts:51` and the test name at `src/core/context.test.ts:176`. `devdocs/settings.md#The model > Precision limit` writes the formula and is clear. Fix: "four times the sum of the precision limit and ten", with the number beside it.
2. **"The passes together cost at most about twice the pass that succeeds, whatever it is."** True while the passes double, since each pass then costs at most about what all before it cost under a cost linear in the digits, and true at the default settings, where the ceiling 4,040 follows 2,304. It fails where the ceiling lies just above a doubled precision: `workingContexts` keeps every doubled precision strictly below the ceiling and then adds the ceiling, so at one digit under a ceiling of 45 the passes are 11, 22, 44, 45 (`src/core/pipeline.test.ts:230`), and at eight digits under a precision limit of 567, a ceiling of 2,308, they are 18 … 2304, 2308: two passes of nearly one size at the end, and the passes together cost about three times the last under a cost linear in the digits. The claim is right in spirit and wrong at that edge. Either the sentence changes (the proposed edit) or `workingContexts` drops a doubled precision that lies within some fraction of the ceiling, after which the sentence is exactly right (question 1). The comment on `DEFAULT_BUDGET_MS` (`src/settings.ts:159-163`) makes the same claim.
3. **"The finest grid the app rounds onto" is not 4,040 digits.** The ceiling is a pass's precision; the grid that pass settles onto is `digitsFor(bitsFor(4040))`, 4,050 digits, the kernel's 32 guard bits being worth 9.6 digits. Where: `docs/settings.md:111-112`, `docs/settings.md:158` ("The finest grid must fit inside it", A5's entry), `devdocs/settings.md#The model > Precision limit` and `> The two limits meet in the grid` and the probe's proposal line (V5's and D4's, question 5), and §8's gloss of "ceiling". The derivation in `> The two limits meet in the grid` is nonetheless right, since it goes through `bitsFor(4 × (L + 10))`, the bits and not the digits. Fix for the user docs: gloss the ceiling as the highest precision any pass computes at, and keep "grid" out of them; in devdocs, say that the ceiling pass's grid is `digitsFor(bitsFor(ceiling))`, about ten digits above the ceiling (the proposed addition under `#Working precision and the schedule`).
4. **"The panel's line says the limit's own top under the significand limit and the finest grid as numbers, never as a formula."** `precisionLimitLine` (`src/settings-lines.ts:147-152`) says "From 1 to 2,453 digits, up to the significand limit. The most digits an answer may be asked for; it bounds the effort an expression may cost." and in decimal mode "The most digits an answer, or the working digits, may be asked for; it bounds the effort a step's rounding may cost."; the module's comment (`src/settings-lines.ts:143-145`) leaves "the grid and the ceiling" to the guide. The range is there and the ceiling is not; the one line that writes the ceiling is the probe's proposal (`src/settings-lines.ts:243-249`, "the built-ins on a grid of 4,040 digits"). The sentence is deleted in the proposed edit; `devdocs/settings.md#The model`'s "derived and shown" is V5's (question 3).
5. **"`checkContext` holds … in decimal mode `working >= precision` and `4 (working + GUARD_DIGITS) <= ceiling`."** The check runs whenever the field is present, in either mode (`src/core/context.ts:298-309`; `src/core/context.test.ts:237-242`, "The same bounds in exact mode, where the field is carried and ignored").
6. **"Every memo of the core is filled on first use, none at module load or at the table's build."** The memo of `pi` is filled by the table's build, which is its first use (the five definitions above call `pi()`); the sentence as written is false.
7. **The issue link in `#Memoized constants`** ("the memo's footprint is #103", `devdocs/numbers.md:383-384`) concerns the Bernoulli memo, which `devdocs/builtins.md#The Gamma and Beta families` owns; by §12 question 26 the link belongs there alone, and the page's reference-style link definitions become inline links (V1).

Of the cost claims in the sections, the qualitative ones hold ("a value that certifies costs milliseconds; one that never certifies runs to the ceiling"; "rebuilding at each pass's precision would cost milliseconds per pass"; "a limit on effort"; "runs all the way up on every keystroke") and stay; "about 1.7 KB per constant" is a size derived from the bits, not a timing, and stays; the one measurement, "about 25 ms at the defaults", leaves (§12, question 25; the stale-passage row).

It caps the display digits and, in decimal mode, the working digits, and it bounds the effort an expression may cost: in exact mode the app computes at a precision a little above the digits asked for and, while the digits asked for are not all certified, computes again at twice the precision, and so on up to a ceiling, four times the sum of the limit and ten digits (4,040 at the default). An expression whose digits can never all be certified (`(2^(1/2))^2 - 2`, which is zero within a bound) runs every pass up to the ceiling on every keystroke, which is what makes the limit a limit on effort; in decimal mode a step's rounding is certified at precisions up to the same ceiling. Changing the limit rebuilds the unit table, since the angle units carry `pi()` computed at the ceiling; changing the display digits never does.

## Terminology

- **"ceiling"**: §8 glosses it "the finest grid the app rounds onto". In the code the ceiling is the last pass's precision (`NumberContext.ceiling`), and the grid of that pass is `digitsFor(bitsFor(ceiling))`, about ten digits above it. Proposed gloss: "the highest precision any pass computes at"; "grid" stays a devdocs word.
- **"working digits"**: three senses in the pages. §8 settles two of them (the decimal-mode setting is "working digits"; a pass's precision is "working precision") and not the third, `workingDigits(p)`, the digits a pass settles onto, which `#Settling` and `#The kernel's boundary` also call "the working digits". Proposed: "the working grid" for `digitsFor(bitsFor(p))` in devdocs, never in docs.
- **"the guard"**: the schedule's `GUARD_DIGITS` (ten digits) and the kernel's `GUARD_BITS` (32 bits) are each "the guard" in `devdocs/numbers.md`; `#Working precision and the schedule` tells them apart in one sentence. Proposed: "the schedule's guard" and "the kernel's guard bits" wherever both are within reach of a sentence.
- **"unaffordable"** (a pass a `tooLarge` marks unaffordable): devdocs-only, used once. Proposed: keep, devdocs-only.
