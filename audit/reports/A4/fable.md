## Findings

The model of N17 to N20 holds against the code in its structure. There is one entrance through which a computed value becomes a value of the expression, `step` of `src/core/ball.ts:1109-1133`, and nothing else rounds: in decimal mode it runs the computation at the working contexts of the retry schedule in exact mode until the ball is exact or certifies the step's digits, then rounds once by `settleDecimal` (`src/core/ball.ts:1052-1081`), which rounds an exact value in the mode, takes a certified rounding as exact, and otherwise takes the midpoint's rounding and sets the flag. The pipeline's schedule is the user's context alone in decimal mode (`src/core/pipeline.ts:299-303`); `resolve` runs one pass, and an `unresolved` is final there. The flag is carried by every ball operation (`carried`, `src/core/ball.ts:321-327`), by `stepQuantity` from a call's arguments to its result (`src/core/quantity.ts:104-114`), by the pipeline's quotients and by the reciprocal line, which is a `step` too (`src/outcome.ts:332`). A built-in never sees a decimal-mode context (`step` hands it exact-mode contexts), and `approx` alone declares itself the identity (`src/core/builtin.ts:175-182`, `src/core/builtins/approx.ts:41`). `operandContext` is the context at the working digits in decimal mode and the context itself otherwise, idempotent, and exact mode returns the context untouched (`src/core/context.ts:119-129`; checked by running it); `checkContext` admits working digits of 1000 under the default precision limit and refuses 1001 and any count below the display digits (`src/core/context.ts:298-308`). `contextOf` hands the context the working digits only when extended (`src/settings.ts:814-826`), and their range is the display digits to the precision limit (`src/settings.ts:509-514`), as the `> Working digits` bullet says.

What the unit corrects or adds, in the order of the claims:

- **The step list** (§1.1.6, item 2). The nodes are as the pages list them, with one omission: a leading `-` is a node and a step (`src/core/evaluate.ts:649-663`), harmless without working digits and the step rounded to `p` with them (`-(1/3*3)` over twelve digits is `= -1`). The literal is rounded as it is read (`src/core/evaluate.ts:520-523`, the number node stepped at its context), after the significand limit has refused one that passes it, deliberately in either mode (`src/core/evaluate.ts:510-518`): a literal of twenty thousand digits is refused in decimal mode, where the page says a literal is rounded. A prefixed unit is one product: the lookup multiplies the prefix's factor and the unit as built, at full precision (`src/core/table.ts:199-203`), and the unit node steps the product once (`src/core/evaluate.ts:531-534`).
- **The uncertified fallback** (N18) is reachable from the shipped built-ins through `log`, whose exact cases recognize an integer exponent or its reciprocal and no other rational (`src/core/builtins/log.ts:17-24`). The flag then behaves as the page says: `log(4, 8) m` in `in` at one digit writes both lines with the dotted sign and the note, `2 log(4, 8)` and `log(4, 8) - log(4, 8)` carry it on. A directed mode certifies the same value, since the `p`-digit value `1.125` is not a boundary of the three-digit grid under toward zero, and one more display digit makes it exact. The page's reasoning that only roots and powers can sit on a boundary assumed every rational value of a built-in is an exact case; the logarithm's are not.
- **The last steps** (§1.1.6, item 3). The list holds for a blank `want` and for the ratio, the terms, the count and the reciprocal, with two refinements. With a `want`, the `have` text is an operand, evaluated at the working digits (`src/core/pipeline.ts:502-503`), so its root is rounded to `w` and only the ratio to `p`: `1 / 3 * 3 m` in `in` moves from `39.370078` to `39.370079` when the working digits are extended. The reciprocal line is `1 / value` of the ratio already rounded to `p` (`src/outcome.ts:332`), so it is correctly rounded from the shown ratio and not from the exact `want / have`: `3 m` in `in` shows `0.0084666664` with or without working digits, where exact mode certifies `0.0084666667`. The directed-mode neighbour claim is confirmed (`0.099999999` toward zero, `0.10000001` away from zero).
- **The display**. The pages' `= -100000000` is written `= -1e+8`, the display putting a nine-digit integer in exponent notation at eight digits. The notes a decimal-mode line never carries are worded "n of m digits are certain", "within … of zero" and "the digits are not certified" (`src/outcome.ts:504-517`); the page quotes a "no digit certain" that no note says.
- **Two consequences of the mode that no page states**, both calculator-like and both candidates for one sentence in the user docs: a unit that carries `pi()` is rounded as it is looked up, so `turn` in `deg` is `= 359.99999 deg` with `deg = 0.0027777779 turn` where exact mode has `≈ 360 deg`; and no argument straddles an edge, so `tan(90 deg)` is `= -13660250` (the degree is rounded first) where exact mode refuses it at the pole, and `asin(1)` is `= 1.5707963 radian`. Likewise every step takes the directed mode, so `1/7` under ceiling is `= 0.14285715`.

The `> Working digits` bullet of `docs/settings.md` holds in every statement: the key `mode.working`, locked or `extended` with a count (`src/settings.ts:121-133`), the default locked (`src/settings.ts:154-156`), the count's range (`src/settings.ts:509-514`), the rule, both examples and the directed-mode neighbour. The `> Mode` bullet holds except for the example and the operator list above, and its "the panel offers the modes as two choices, `exact` and `decimal`, and says under them what the one in force does" matches `MODE_LINES` of `src/settings-lines.ts:259-264`.

## Questions for the owner

1. **The reciprocal line in decimal mode** is `1 / value` of the shown `p`-digit ratio (`src/outcome.ts:332`), so with extended working digits it is not the correctly rounded reciprocal of the exact ratio (`3 m` in `in`: `0.0084666664` shown, `0.0084666667` exact).
