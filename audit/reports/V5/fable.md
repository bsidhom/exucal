**Two statements the code contradicts.**

1. `devdocs/notation.md#The rule`, rule 3, says a non-integer exact value is written as its fraction "when the numerator and the denominator have at most `W` digits between them", and the page's own constraint is that "the output must be predictable from the value and the settings, never from anything internal to the calculation". `ratioParts` (`src/core/display.ts:131-156`) forms the fraction only when the canonical exponent `k` lies within `2W` and the canonical numerator has at most `2W` digits, on the reasoning in its comment that a denominator keeps at least `5^-k` of the `10^-k`. That reasoning assumes the numerator coprime to ten, but the canonical form only keeps it not divisible by ten (`src/core/scaled.ts:8-11`): a numerator divisible by `5^-k` cancels the fives and the denominator keeps `2^-k`, which has far fewer digits. So at the default width `2^-41` through `2^-63`, `3/2^45` and `1/(7*2^41)` are written as rounded decimals with `≈` though their ratios fit the width, while `2^-40` is written `= 1/1099511627776`. The rule, the summary paragraph and N13 are right as design; the code's screen is the defect. No test covers the boundary. Question 1 proposes the remedy.
2. `devdocs/notation.md#Where a ratio is written` says the reciprocal's ratio is parenthesized, `in = (1/36) × 3 ft`, for the reader's sake. The printer writes the fewest parentheses that read the tree back (`src/core/print.ts:5-6`, `:14-23`), so the line is `in = 1/36 × 3 ft` and `in = 127/5000 × 1 m` (`src/outcome.test.ts:87-98`, and the harness), while a ratio before a juxtaposed factor is parenthesized (`in = (-127/5000) (-1 m)`, `:111-113`). The same paragraph and `#Where it lives` attribute the parentheses to the outcome; the outcome composes the expression and the printer decides. `docs/settings.md` repeats the parenthesized form (question 2).

## Proposed edits

### devdocs/notation.md#(top)

Old (first two paragraphs) → new:

In exact mode an exact value may be written entire, as an integer or as a ratio of two integers in lowest terms, rather than as its digits rounded to the display digits: `1 m = (5000/127) in` rather than `≈ 39.370079 in`. The Notation entry of [docs/settings.md](../docs/settings.md) says what a user sees; this page is the rule, its reasons and where it lives. The notation is a setting of exact mode (`digits`, the default, or `ratio` with a width; [settings.md](settings.md)), and it is a display alone: the value is what the pipeline computed, the radius says whether it is exact, and the setting says how an exact value is written. The two displays never disagree about what is exact, since both read the radius, and nothing in the core computes differently under either.

The decimal display writes a value's digits rounded to the display digits in the rounding mode and says `=` only when the radius is zero and every digit held is shown ([numbers.md](numbers.md), "Certification and the display rule"), so an exact value with more digits than the display digits is written with `≈` and no note: `1 m` in `in` is `≈ 39.370079 in`, though the value is exactly `5000/127` inches. The display is right that the digits shown are not the whole value, and it has no way to show the whole value, which has no last digit. A ratio has: `5000/127` is the value entire, in ten characters, and it parses back as the same value.

## The model

[docs/numbers.md](../docs/numbers.md) says how a value is held, to the depth a user needs: the significand and its bits, the power of ten and its bound, the error radius, the digits an approximate value is kept to, and the two limits that bound them; [docs/settings.md](../docs/settings.md) says what each setting does and links there. This section is the model behind the settings: what each field of `Settings` is in the core's terms, why the limits are named and measured as they are, how the two limits meet, the chain that keeps the settings consistent, and the shapes of the model that are ruled out.

**The fields.** `Settings.displayDigits` is `NumberContext.precision`, the digits an answer is shown with and, in decimal mode, the grid of every step; the working precision of a pass in exact mode is the schedule's and never the user's ([numbers.md](numbers.md), "Working precision and the schedule"), so the display digits are not split into digits shown and digits computed with. `Settings.mode.working`, decimal mode's working digits, is `NumberContext.working` when extended, the grid of every observable step but the last ([numbers.md](numbers.md), "The working digits"); a built-in is one observable step, and inside it the kernel rounds onto finer grids, up to the ceiling, until its rounding onto the working grid is certified, so the working digits bound what a user can see and not what a built-in may use to behave as one operation. `Settings.precisionLimitDigits` derives `NumberContext.ceiling` through `ceilingFor`, `4 × (L + 10)` digits for a limit `L`, the finest grid the app rounds onto in either mode, and has its own top under the significand limit from `precisionLimitFor`; the ceiling is derived and shown, never set and never written as a formula. `Settings.significandLimitBits` is `NumberContext.significandLimitBits`, the most bits in a significand's numerator and, separately, in its denominator; an exact result past it is refused and named, and a grid point, being a significand, must fit inside it. `Settings.budgetMs` is the probe's alone ("The probe" below), stored with the rest and never seen by the core; its range, from 10 ms, below which no candidate fits, to 10,000, ten seconds a pass being past any use, is the probe's too (`BUDGET_MS_RANGE`). `Settings.mode.notation` is a display setting that reaches `show` and the outcome and nothing below them ([notation.md](notation.md)). Every number carries its unit, on screen after its input and in every line ("1,000 digits", "65,536 bits", "50 ms", a count of one as "1 digit") and in the stored key (`displayDigits`, `precisionLimitDigits`, `significandLimitBits`, `budgetMs`), so that no name has to carry it and no number is read without it.

**The names.** "Significand" is IEEE 754's word for the part an exponent scales, and it is the word everywhere in the code and the docs over "mantissa": IEEE 754 moved away from that word, and it also names the fractional part of a logarithm. "Precision" is the word the numeric libraries use for the digit count of the working grid (mpmath's `dps`, the `decimal` context's precision), and the precision limit is the cap on that; "digits limit" would suggest a cap on the display digits and nothing else, "grid limit" would name the ceiling grid itself, and "grid precision" reads as a value rather than a bound and collides with the working digits. The significand limit says the part of the representation it bounds; "size limit" says nothing about what is sized, and "most bits" names the unit and not the thing. Its unit is right only with care: the limit `S` applies to each part on its own, so a significand at the limit holds up to `2S` bits, but a claim of `2S` bits of precision would promise more than the representation holds (only reduced representations are admitted, so the values a significand of `S` bits per part can take number fewer than `2^(2S)`), while `S` bits is a lower bound on the precision reachable within the limit (an integer of `S` bits is such a significand), so the setting says `S` bits and claims nothing about `2S`. The translation to decimal digits is per part and never a count of the value's digits: `1/3` has no digit count of its own, and the sheet's line gives the digits of one part at three significant figures, following the limit in force (`digitsOfBits`). A reader of mpmath or MPFR, where the precision is the width of a binary significand and there is nothing else, should not expect the limit to coincide with the precision limit.

**The two limits meet in the grid.** A grid point is itself a significand, an integer over one, which is what ties the two limits together. A grid of `d` digits takes `bitsFor(d) = ceil(d × log2 10) + 32` significand bits in the kernel, the 32 being the guard (`GUARD_BITS`); the finest grid under a precision limit `L` is `4 × (L + 10)` digits; and every ball operation forms its result from its operands' significands under the significand limit, inexact operands included, the worst case being a product of two grid points on the ceiling grid. So `2 × bitsFor(4 × (L + 10)) ≤ S`, and `precisionLimitFor(S)` is the largest `L` that satisfies it: 2,453 at the default `S`, where 2,454 fails; the setting's floor, `MIN_SIGNIFICAND_LIMIT_BITS`, is the least `S` that holds a limit of one digit, `2 × bitsFor(ceilingFor(1))`. Read one way, a small significand limit forces a smaller precision limit; read the other, a precision limit needs a significand limit of at least twice its ceiling grid's bits. An exact value is bounded by the significand limit alone (it is never rounded, so no digits setting touches it, and the display writes as many of its digits as the display digits ask for and marks the rest rounded away); an inexact value is bounded by the precision limit alone (its significand is a grid point, so its size is the grid's, and it is never refused for size).

**The chain.** Significand limit → precision limit → display digits → working digits, each clamped by the one above it, the working digits last and by both their bounds (the display digits their floor, the precision limit their cap). The ceiling is derived from the precision limit, and so is the precision limit's own top under the significand limit. The ratio notation's width (`[1, 1000]`) and the probe's budget depend on nothing and nothing clamps them. A stored entry is therefore never inconsistent.

The three numbers stay settings, with the clamps as they are, and the ceiling is derived and shown, never set. Two other shapes are ruled out. Deriving the precision limit from the significand limit, keeping one cost setting, loses because the two bound different things that cost differently (a product of two significands is quadratic in bits, a built-in's ceiling pass is in digits of a different operation), the probe measures them apart because of it, and a user on a device fast at one and slow at the other would have no way to say so. Making the ceiling the setting, the finest grid itself, with the most digits an answer may be asked for derived from it, is the honest pairing with the significand limit, since the inequality above becomes direct and it is the quantity the mechanism uses; it loses because the number a user has a reason to set is how many digits an answer may be asked for, the schedule's room for certification would then surface as the derivation of that maximum instead of the ceiling, and the default, 4,040, is no one's choice. It is recorded as the alternative, should the setting ever be re-based on the grid. The ceiling is one field of the context, read in both modes: in decimal mode every step whose exact result is not representable is computed on rising grids up to the ceiling until its rounding is certified, so the limit has one meaning in both modes.
